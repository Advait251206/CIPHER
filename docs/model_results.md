## 2. Phishing URL model (PhiUSIIL, Random Forest)

Points a reviewer will raise, and our position:

- **INFILTRATION and OTHER_ATTACK (Heartbleed) have single-digit test support.** Their per-class numbers are anecdotal and are not claimed as results.
- **BOTNET precision is about 70%.** ARES bot traffic overlaps with benign polling in flow statistics. The `HEUR-ANOMALY-002` IRC-port rule and IOC matching are the intended cross-checks.
- **Day split evaluates generalization to unseen attack categories.** Training on Monday–Thursday and testing on Friday drops binary accuracy from 99.90% to 77.38% (recall 37.08%) and per-class F1 for Friday attacks to 0.0000. This is not primarily IP memorization (the model has no IP features), but zero-shot category absence: Friday's attacks (DDoS, PortScan, Botnet) never appear in Monday–Thursday training. It measures how tree ensembles fail when facing novel attack categories they were never trained to classify.
- **Known dataset defects.** Engelen, Rimmer & Joosen (IEEE SPW 2021) report CICFlowMeter bugs and labelling errors in CIC-IDS2017 and publish a corrected version. Re-running on it is recommended.
- **Preprocessing consistency.** Training replaces +inf with each file's 99.9th percentile, while inference uses 1e6. Imputation medians were computed before the split. Both should be fitted on the training partition only.

## 4. Real-world URL probe (raw model vs. full service)

`python -m training.probe_phishing` scores 15 legitimate deep links and 10 phishing patterns.

| Measure | Baseline Model (phiusiil-rf-v1) | Retrained Model (Tranco deep paths, probe-excluded) |
| --- | :---: | :---: |
| Raw model legitimate FP (P ≥ 0.5) | 15 / 15 (100%) | 9 / 15 (60.0%) |
| Raw model phishing misses | 1 / 10 (`www.paypa1.com`) | 2 / 10 |
| Full service legitimate FP | 2 / 15 (`docs.python.org`, `sbi.co.in`) | 0 / 15 (0.0%) |
| Full service phishing misses | 1 / 10 (`www.paypa1.com`) | 2 / 10 |

**Whitelisting vs. Raw ML.** The full service achieves 0/15 false positives primarily through the two-tier domain whitelist (`TRUSTED_DOMAINS` and the Tranco top-50k list), because most legitimate domains in the probe reside in Tranco. The true statistical capability of the machine learning classifier alone is reflected in the raw model row (which dropped from 15/15 to 9/15), not the whitelist-assisted service verdict.

**Root Cause & Architectural Trade-offs.**
- *Bare-homepage bias in PhiUSIIL*: All 134,850 legitimate URLs in raw PhiUSIIL are bare homepages (`https://www.example.com`), whereas phishing URLs carry paths, subdomains, and query strings. The baseline model learned a naive decision boundary: any deep path indicates phishing.
- *The consequence of path augmentation*: Retraining with 25,000 synthetic deep paths from diverse Tranco domains (excluding probe domains) successfully forced the tree ensemble to evaluate lexical structure rather than merely penalizing the presence of a path, dropping raw false alarms from 15/15 to 9/15.
- *The new vulnerability: phishing on legitimate / user-publishing platforms*: Training the model on thousands of diverse hosts with generic paths taught the decision trees that ordinary-looking paths (e.g., `/view/...`, `/login`, `/account`) are typical of legitimate websites. As a direct consequence, the retrained model exhibits reduced sensitivity to phishing hosted on legitimate cloud platforms and user-content services. For instance, `sites.google.com/view/paypal-verify-account` dropped from a threat score of 85 (`LIKELY_PHISHING`) to 21 (`LEGITIMATE`), and `evil.weebly.com/office365-login` received a raw model probability of only 0.09.
- *Independent evaluation on unseen paths*: In testing on held-out URLs with paths not seen in training (measured during external review, Oct 31, 2026), the raw model flagged 7 out of 15 legitimate URLs (including deep paths on `mit.edu`, `nist.gov`, `bbc.co.uk`, `rust-lang.org`, `cloudflare.com`, `flipkart.com`, and `sbi.co.in`) and missed 1 out of 12 phishing URLs. This demonstrates that pure lexical URL classification has intrinsic limitations: distinguishing an authentic corporate login page from a cloud-hosted credential harvest cannot be solved reliably by URL string features alone without external context (e.g. host domain reputation, TLS certificates, or DOM content).

## 5. Email phishing model

- Dataset: six Kaggle corpora (CEAS-08, Enron, Ling, Nazario, Nigerian Fraud, SpamAssassin); 70/15/15 split stratified by label.
- Model: Random Forest (100 trees, depth 20) on 32 engineered features. Test metrics are in the summary table.
- **Caveat.** The corpora differ in era and source, and the top features are length and style (`avg_word_length`, `body_length`, `exclamation_count`), so the model may partly identify the corpus rather than the intent. The leave-one-corpus-out (LOCO) evaluation in section 5.1 below documents this generalization gap across each corpus.
